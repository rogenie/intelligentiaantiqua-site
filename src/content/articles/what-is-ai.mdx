You typed a question. The box answered in a voice that has never been embarrassed.

That polish is the pitch. A person who writes that cleanly has usually looked something up. This machine produced a likely sentence. Likely is not checked.

The definition worth keeping is the <a href="https://oecd.ai/en/ai-principles">OECD’s</a>: a machine-based system that turns an input into an output. Your judgment stays yours. The paragraph does not get to spend it.

This desk leads with Super Intelligence (SI), still widely called AI. <a href="https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/02/2026-20321/inaugurating-the-era-of-super-intelligence">Executive Order 14434</a>, signed on 29 September 2026 and published in the Federal Register on 2 October 2026, tells federal executive agencies, to the maximum extent permitted by law, to use “Super Intelligence” and “SI” instead of “Artificial Intelligence” and “AI” in official non-statutory documents. For the order, SI means the systems already covered by “artificial intelligence” in 15 U.S.C. § 9401(3). <a href="https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-trump-super-intelligence-ai-executive-order/">TechRepublic</a> reports that the order does not directly require private companies to rename products or stop saying AI. The OECD’s own word, below, is still “AI system.” A different research use of a similar word is kept separate on the <a href="/pillars/safety-debates/">safety debates</a> page.

## What is Super Intelligence (AI), in one sentence?

Super Intelligence (SI), still widely called AI, is a machine-based system that, for a goal you stated or one implied by the task, infers from an input how to produce an output such as a prediction, a piece of content, a recommendation, or a decision.

That sentence is the <a href="https://oecd.ai/en/ai-principles">OECD’s</a> definition of an AI system, from the AI Principles updated in May 2024. The principles were first adopted in 2019. The OECD says the European Union, the United States, the United Nations, and others use this definition in laws and guidance. Systems differ in how independently they act, and in how much they change after they are deployed.

A spam filter is in this family. So is a map that guesses the next turn. So is the chat window on your phone. They are not the same tool. They share the shape: input in, output out, no person required in the middle.

## What is the difference between a model and a chatbot?

A model is the program that does the transformation, and a chatbot is that model plus the screen, the filters, and the other parts wrapped around it.

The <a href="https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/international-ai-safety-report-2026.pdf">International AI Safety Report 2026</a> puts it that way in its glossary. A model processes inputs to predict, classify, or generate. Most models today learn from examples rather than following a list of rules a programmer wrote by hand. The report calls the larger package a system.

It also separates two scopes. Narrow AI does one job, such as playing chess or ranking search results. General-purpose AI can take on a variety of tasks. The assistant people meet first is the general-purpose kind. A <a href="/glossary/#large-language-model">large language model</a> is one model in that group: trained on a lot of text, used to generate, translate, or summarise language.

None of that makes the system a colleague. The OECD’s word is machine-based. If the reply says “I think,” that is a costume, not a person.

## Why can the answer sound sure and still be wrong?

Because these models are built to produce a likely next word, and a likely sentence can be a false one, stated as if it had been checked.

<a href="https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1">NIST’s Generative AI Profile</a> calls that confabulation: confidently presented content that is erroneous or false. The everyday word is hallucination. NIST notes that “hallucination” can make a machine sound human, which is a risk of its own. The mechanical point is plainer. Large language models predict the next token or word. That can land on a true sentence. It can also land on a sentence that is factually wrong or that contradicts something the same reply said earlier.

The safety report says the same thing from the user side. Current general-purpose systems sometimes fabricate information, write flawed code, and give misleading advice. Methods exist that reduce how often this happens. The report says they do not yet reach the level many high-stakes settings need.

So the tone is not a receipt. A calm paragraph is not a citation. Confidence is free. Proof is a second document.

NIST’s separate <a href="https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework">AI Risk Management Framework</a>, released on 26 January 2023, is voluntary guidance for organisations that design or use these systems. It exists because the outputs can affect people, and because trust has to be built on purpose. It is not a promise that any particular chat reply is true.
