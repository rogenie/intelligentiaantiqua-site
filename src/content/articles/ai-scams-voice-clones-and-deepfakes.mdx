The phone rings. The voice is your grandson, or your boss, and the story wants money before you can think. Bail. A hospital. A wire that has to leave in the next ten minutes.

Panic is the product. The voice is no longer proof. This page is the check that does not depend on your ear, and the steps stay plain.

The <a href="https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/international-ai-safety-report-2026.pdf">International AI Safety Report 2026</a> says scammers already use generated voices and video to push people to send money. It also says public counts of how often this happens are still thin. We will not invent a total.

## What is a voice clone or a deepfake?

A deepfake is AI-generated audio or visual content that shows a person saying or doing something they did not say or do, or an event that did not happen. A voice clone is the audio version of that trick.

That definition is the safety report’s. The report says malicious actors use general-purpose systems to make realistic fake content for scams, extortion, and manipulation. It describes documented cases of executives authorising large transfers to fraudsters, and of ordinary people sending smaller amounts to someone posing as a loved one. It does not give a single dollar figure we can repeat as “the” loss, so this page will not invent one.

The same section says AI has made it easier to produce that fake content at scale, including non-consensual sexual images. That harm is real, and it is not the same incident as a family-emergency call. Both sit under the report’s heading of AI-generated content used to hurt people.

A US phone rule sits beside this, and it is narrower than “all fake voices are a crime in every context.” In a declaratory ruling released on 8 February 2024, the <a href="https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-17A1.pdf">FCC</a> confirmed that the Telephone Consumer Protection Act’s restrictions on an “artificial or prerecorded voice” cover current AI technologies that generate human voices. A call that uses those technologies needs the called person’s prior express consent, unless the call is for an emergency purpose or an exemption applies. The ruling is about robocalls. It does not train your ear.

## Why is listening a weak test?

Because studies the safety report cites found that people often accept a clone as the real speaker, and that a warning label on a video still leaves most viewers wrong.

The <a href="https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/international-ai-safety-report-2026.pdf">report</a> says one audio study found that listeners took AI voice clones for the real speaker in 80 percent of cases. On video, a study of warning labels moved accuracy at spotting AI-generated video from 10.7 percent to 21.6 percent. Most people in that study still missed the fake. The report also says humans often do no better than chance at identifying deepfakes, and that detection tools and labels have mixed results. A benchmark that looks good in a lab can do much worse on fakes from the wild.

So “it sounded exactly like her” is what the scam is designed to produce. It is not a verification step.

## What should you do when the call asks for money?

Do not pay from the call. Hang up, and reach the person on a number you already had.

The report shows why the voice is not the check. The habit follows from that finding. Urgency is part of the pattern the report describes: the caller wants the transfer before you have talked to anyone else. Give yourself that conversation.

Use this order:

1. Do not send money, read a code aloud, or switch to a payment app because the voice is familiar.
2. Hang up. Call the person using a number already in your phone, or ask another relative you can reach without using a number the caller just dictated.
3. If the story was false, report it. In the United States, the FBI’s <a href="https://www.ic3.gov/">Internet Crime Complaint Center</a> takes internet-crime complaints, and the FTC takes fraud reports at <a href="https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/">ReportFraud.ftc.gov</a>.

A video call is not a stronger test by itself. The report’s definition of a deepfake includes visual content. Seeing a face on a screen the caller set up is still the caller’s channel.
