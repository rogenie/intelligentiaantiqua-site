The photo of the card is in your hand. The chat box is waiting for the number.

Do not send it. The box will not blush, and it will not forget because you closed the tab.

Super Intelligence (SI), still widely called AI, is the name <a href="https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/02/2026-20321/inaugurating-the-era-of-super-intelligence">Executive Order 14434</a> tells federal agencies to use in place of artificial intelligence. For the order, those words mean the systems already covered by the statutory definition of artificial intelligence. The order is about the name agencies use. It does not set a chat-history switch. A hypothetical model that equals or surpasses human performance on all or almost all cognitive tasks is a different idea. The <a href="https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/international-ai-safety-report-2026.pdf">International AI Safety Report 2026</a> calls that <a href="/glossary/#artificial-general-intelligence">artificial general intelligence</a>. The chatbot on your phone is the ordinary tool. The word for the order's name is in the <a href="/glossary/#super-intelligence">glossary</a>.

On November 9, 2026, the New York Department of State's Division of Consumer Protection told people to stop handing personal information to these chatbots. <a href="https://dos.ny.gov/news/consumer-alert-caution-against-sharing-personal-information-ai-chatbots">The alert</a> is the list. The settings below are the companies' own pages, opened the day this guide was published.

## What should you never paste into an SI (AI) chatbot?

Leave out Social Security numbers, driver's licenses, passport numbers, passwords, PINs, credit-card details, health information, and any photo of those documents.

That list is from Acting ITS Chief Information Officer Jenson Jacob, in the <a href="https://dos.ny.gov/news/consumer-alert-caution-against-sharing-personal-information-ai-chatbots">New York alert</a>. He called never sharing those items the most effective safeguard. The same page also names your name, address, and birth date, account numbers, a medical diagnosis or a lab result, legal documents, and usernames or passwords.

A photograph is not a loophole. Jennifer King, lead author of a Stanford study, said that if you share sensitive information in a dialogue with ChatGPT, Gemini, or other frontier models, it "may be collected and used for training, even if it's in a separate file that you uploaded during the conversation." <a href="https://hai.stanford.edu/news/be-careful-what-you-tell-your-ai-chatbot">Stanford HAI published that account</a> on October 15, 2025. It describes the policies the scholars read then. <a href="https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13594961?hl=en">Google's Gemini Apps Privacy Hub</a> still says that chat content used to train models can include photos, files, and other attachments you share, when that setting is on.

Health notes and work files sit in the same pile. <a href="https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8325621-i-would-like-to-input-sensitive-data-into-my-chats-with-claude-who-can-view-my-conversations">Anthropic's page for consumer Claude</a> tells you to be thoughtful about financial information, health records, passwords or private login credentials, and confidential business or personal documents. A work or school login is a different contract from a personal one. <a href="https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13278892?hl=en">Google</a> says a Workspace administrator controls Gemini history on a work or school account. <a href="https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/privacy/microsoft-copilot/activity-history">Microsoft</a> says its consumer Copilot activity page does not cover a work or school account.

A question about a public form is a different act from pasting the form after the numbers are filled in. <a href="/articles/how-to-use-an-ai-assistant/">How to use a Super Intelligence (AI) assistant safely</a> makes that split for ChatGPT. This page is the longer leave-out list.

## Why might an SI (AI) chatbot store what you type?

A chat can be a record the company keeps, and New York's alert says to assume that anything you give a chatbot may be collected.

The <a href="https://dos.ny.gov/news/consumer-alert-caution-against-sharing-personal-information-ai-chatbots">alert</a> says to assume that any information given to an AI chatbot will be collected and used for training, and that it could be viewed by a human reviewer or hacked. It points to a Stanford study for that caution. <a href="https://hai.stanford.edu/news/be-careful-what-you-tell-your-ai-chatbot">Stanford HAI's October 15, 2025 account</a> says the scholars read privacy documents from six U.S. companies, Amazon, Anthropic, Google, Meta, Microsoft, and OpenAI, and found that all six used chat data to train their models by default. That was a reading of the policies then. It is not a live setting on your screen today.

Secretary of State Walter T. Mosley said in the same alert that sharing sensitive information can increase the risk of identity theft and other financial harms. The alert also says a private or incognito browser mode can limit what stays on your device, and that the information may still be sent to the AI platform.

A training switch is not a delete button. <a href="https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7730893-data-controls-in-chatgpt">OpenAI</a> says that turning off "Improve the model for everyone" does not delete or hide saved chats. <a href="https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13594961?hl=en">Google</a> says that even with Gemini History off, future chats are still saved for around two months, and that chats a human reviewer has seen can be kept for up to three years after you delete history.

## What do the major assistants do with chat history?

Each company writes its own rule, and the rule depends on your plan and the switch you actually see.

Believe the help page if your screen has moved. The <a href="/corrections/">corrections page</a> is there if this guide has gone stale.

**ChatGPT.** On a personal Free, Plus, or Pro account, <a href="https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7730893-data-controls-in-chatgpt">OpenAI</a> says data sharing is on by default. The control is "Improve the model for everyone," under Settings, then Data controls, on the web and in the phone apps. Turning it off keeps your chat history. <a href="https://help.openai.com/en/articles/5722486-how-your-data-is-used-to-improve-model-performance">The companion article</a> says that after you opt out, new conversations are not used to improve the models. A temporary chat is not used to improve the models while it stays temporary, and OpenAI may keep a copy for up to 30 days for safety. If you later tap thumbs-up or thumbs-down, OpenAI says the whole conversation tied to that feedback may still be used to train models. During a Daybreak session on a personal account, OpenAI says it may use that session to improve models even when the training switch is off. Voice mode has its own training control. Business, Enterprise, Edu, and ChatGPT for Healthcare workspaces are not used to train OpenAI's models by default. <a href="https://openai.com/index/how-chatgpt-protects-privacy/">OpenAI's privacy note</a> says not to share sensitive information you would not want used or reviewed. If you are signed out, the same switch is in Settings, and the choice is saved in that browser.

**Claude.** For consumer Free, Pro, and Max plans, including Claude Code on those accounts, <a href="https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/10023580-is-my-data-used-for-model-training">Anthropic</a> says it uses your chats and coding sessions to improve its models if you choose to allow that. The switch is labeled "Help Improve our AI models," under Settings, then Privacy. <a href="https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/12109829-how-do-i-change-my-model-improvement-privacy-settings">The settings article</a> says that turning it off stops new chats, and previously stored chats, from being used in future training runs. Data already inside a training run that has started, or inside a model already trained, stays there. If you allow the use, <a href="https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/10023548-how-long-do-you-store-my-data">Anthropic</a> says it may keep a de-identified copy for up to five years in its training pipelines, for new or resumed chats after the setting is on. Incognito chats are not used to improve Claude, even if the setting is on. A conversation flagged by safety classifiers may still be used for trust and safety work. For Claude for Work and the API, <a href="https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996868-is-my-data-used-for-model-training">Anthropic says</a> it does not, by default, use inputs or outputs to train its models. <a href="https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/10023580-is-my-data-used-for-model-training">On consumer plans</a>, feedback you send with thumbs-up or thumbs-down is stored with the related conversation for up to five years.

**Gemini.** If you are 18 or over and signed in to a personal account, <a href="https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13278892?hl=en">Google</a> says History is on by default, and chat content, including text and attachments, is on by default. Both controls are under Settings, then History, at gemini.google.com. The steps on that page call the checkbox "Chat content including text & uploads." The note above the steps calls it "Chat content including text & attachments." Uncheck it and confirm if you do not want that training. Turning the training settings on requires History to be on. <a href="https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13594961?hl=en">The privacy hub</a> says temporary chats are not used to train Google's AI models. It also says that if History is off and you do not submit feedback, Google does not use your future chats to train generative AI models. Those future chats are still saved for around two months. Reviewed chats can be kept for up to three years even if you delete history. By default, Gemini history older than 18 months is auto-deleted, and you can change that period. A work or school account follows the administrator, not this personal page.

**Copilot.** For the Microsoft Copilot app available as of August 18, 2026, signed in with a personal Microsoft account, <a href="https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/privacy/microsoft-copilot/activity-history">Microsoft</a> says prompts, responses, and your file contents are not used to train foundation models. Chat history is still kept, so you can return to a conversation. Optional feedback may be used to improve Copilot, and Microsoft says that feedback is not used to train those foundation models. If you have not updated to that app, Microsoft says the activity-history page does not apply to you. A work or school account is outside that page. A <a href="/glossary/#prompt">prompt</a> is the input you give the system, and an attached file is part of what you gave it.

## How do you delete a chat you already sent?

Delete it with that product's own control, and read how long a copy can remain, because the training switch does not erase the chat.

Do these three things, in this order.

1. Leave passwords, PINs, ID or card numbers, health details, work files, and photos of documents out of the box.
2. Open the training or history setting for the assistant you use. On personal ChatGPT, turn off "Improve the model for everyone" if you do not want new chats used to train models. On consumer Claude, turn off "Help Improve our AI models" if you do not want that use. On personal Gemini, turn off chat-content training if you do not want it. On the current personal Copilot app, Microsoft says prompts are not used to train foundation models.
3. Delete any chat that already contains something private. Deletion removes it from your history now. The company's page, not the training switch, says whether a copy remains and for how long.

**ChatGPT.** On the web, open the chat's more-options menu in the sidebar, choose Delete, and confirm. <a href="https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8809935-deleting-and-archiving-chats-in-chatgpt">OpenAI</a> says the chat leaves your account view immediately and is scheduled for permanent deletion within 30 days, unless it was already de-identified or OpenAI must keep it for security or legal reasons. You cannot restore it. Archive is not delete. On the phone, press and hold the chat title, then Delete.

**Claude.** On the web, hover the conversation, open the menu, choose Delete, and confirm. <a href="https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8230524-delete-or-rename-a-conversation">Anthropic</a> says it is removed from your history immediately and deleted from back-end systems within 30 days. <a href="https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/10023548-how-long-do-you-store-my-data">If you had allowed training</a>, a deleted chat will not be used to train future models, and a training run already in progress can still include it.

**Gemini.** Go to gemini.google.com, open Settings, then History, and delete one item or choose Delete, then All time. <a href="https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13278892?hl=en">Google</a> says that starts removal from the product and its systems. <a href="https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13594961?hl=en">The privacy hub</a> says a chat a reviewer has already seen is not removed by that action and can be kept for up to three years.

**Copilot.** Sign in with your Microsoft account, open the chat in the Chats list, choose More, then Delete. <a href="https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/privacy/microsoft-copilot/privacy-controls">Microsoft</a> says that removes the conversation from your chat history. To clear all activity, use the privacy-dashboard path on that same page.
