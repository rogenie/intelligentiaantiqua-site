# Intelligentia Antiqua style guide

The personality is [brand-voice.md](brand-voice.md): bold, exacting, playful, warm to the curious, feminine and freedom-loving. Goddess Rogenie sets that voice. This page is the shape a file has to take so the quality gate can check it. Where the two seem to pull apart, the voice stays, and the safety instruction stays calm.

## Voice

Short paragraphs. One idea, then a break. Second person is welcome. Speak like a person who has already decided not to be impressed by a pitch. Take the beginner’s question seriously. Do not talk down.

Use this shape for every article:

1. **Headline.** A clear promise of understanding. It can stand alone in a search result.
2. **Opening hook.** A moment the reader recognizes: a chat box, a phone call, a setting they have not opened.
3. **Problem.** Name what is confusing or risky before you teach the mechanism.
4. **The cited fact.** Make the cost of guessing concrete with a source. Do not invent a named victim, a dollar loss, or a countdown.
5. **What to do.** A checklist or an explanation in short paragraphs. Question headings. The first sentence answers the question.
6. **Pictures.** A featured image, plus at least two diagrams that teach a fact the prose also states. Original SVG. Descriptive alt text.
7. **One next step.** The next page on the start-here path. No buy button. There is no product yet.

Plain words. Define jargon in the sentence or send the reader to the glossary.

### What the voice must not do

- No promise that a chatbot is correct, that a job is safe, or that a job is gone, unless a cited source says that exact thing.
- No fake urgency: no "act now," "limited time," "last chance," "don't miss out," or "before it's too late." You may describe a scammer's pressure, with a citation. You may not add your own.
- No hype phrases. The quality gate rejects `guaranteed`, `unhackable`, `foolproof`, `get rich`, `risk-free`, and `risk free`, including in negation.
- Every factual claim still has an inline link. Every `##` section needs one.
- The page includes the sentence "not professional advice."
- Stories are patterns from a cited source. Do not invent a person, a city, or a loss amount.
- Where a source says experts disagree, the page says experts disagree.
- The newsletter block is a placeholder. It does not collect an address.

Home, hub, glossary, and policy pages can be bolder than the guides. The guides keep the joke out of any step a person might follow while scared or in a hurry. See [brand-voice.md](brand-voice.md).

## Visual system

Stone, parchment, and serif type, with the grace of a seal rather than a dashboard. Thin gold rules, italic used on purpose, room around the words. Fast because the type is a system font stack, not a downloaded family. The look is feminine and self-possessed. It is not pastel, and it is not a startup template.

| Role | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stone | `#241E18` | Header, wordmark, dark bands |
| Bronze | `#6B3A24` | Primary buttons. Cream text `#F4EFE6` on this bronze. |
| Parchment | `#F4EFE6` | Page background |
| Ink | `#1C1612` | Body text |
| Secondary text | `#4A433C` | Notes and captions on parchment |
| Link | `#1D4A38` | Link text on parchment |
| Link, dark mode | `#E2C39A` | Link text on `#14110E` |
| Warning | `#8A341F` | Scam labels on light backgrounds |

Illustrations are original SVG. No stock photos. No glowing chips, no robots with faces. Article diagrams are lazy-loaded SVG files with width, height, and a real alt string. The home hero is inline SVG.

The wordmark is an IA monogram cut like a small stone seal, plus the name Intelligentia Antiqua. “Antiqua” is italic.
